THE REFLECTION

Some of the most meaningful music I've written wasn't born from happiness. It was born from love. From loss. From pain. From missing someone. Perhaps all of it is simply energy in motion. Emotions are the guideposts of experience.

I've come to realise that grief doesn't only bring sadness. It changes the way we see. The way we feel. Sometimes, the way we create.

Every composition tells its own story. But every life experience leaves something behind, that quietly becomes part of the music. I think of my mother-in-law. Living with Alzheimer's, in her final years. I would play the piano for her, and she would react, be moved… sometimes to tears. Then I would play the very same piece again, days later, and she would react exactly the same way. Not because she remembered it. Because she didn't.

For most of us, music grows more meaningful the more we return to it. Layer upon layer, memory upon memory. But for her, every time was the first time. And somehow, it moved her every time, as though the music didn't need memory to reach her at all.

Perhaps grief never truly leaves us. Perhaps, with time, it simply changes its voice.
